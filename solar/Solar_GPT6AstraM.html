Two scales

True scale uses AU for every position and radius. Most planets are subpixel from the overview; name markers help you find them. Teaching scale maps heliocentric distance to 3√r, enlarges radii and separates moon orbits. Measurements always use physical coordinates. Comet tails are anti-solar in both views.

Physics

Kepler: safeguarded Newton–Raphson with a bracketed fallback. Eight planets use JPL's J2000 base elements, held fixed. Dwarf planets and comets use Horizons osculating elements evaluated at JD 2451545.0. Moons use approximate mean orbits; giant-planet moons use their parent's equator. The Moon includes approximate nodal and apsidal precession.

N-body: Sun + eight planets, barycentric positions and velocities, Newtonian gravity, velocity Verlet at a fixed 0.125-day step. Smaller bodies remain on prescribed orbits. Work is capped per frame; at very high requested speeds simulation time slows rather than enlarging the step. A second-order substep estimate keeps low-speed motion continuous. The display reports actual speed and relative energy drift. Changing date reinitializes the integration.

What is illustrative?

Surfaces are seeded fractal gradient noise, not mapped geography. Atmospheres, corona, cloud motion and ring scattering are approximations. Eclipse shadows use real physical sphere geometry even in teaching scale, but simplified moon ephemerides cannot predict historical eclipses. The solar/lunar eclipse buttons search this model for a near alignment within the next two years and pause on it; these are model demonstrations, not historical event dates. Belt particles are statistical samples: Kirkwood gaps, Hilda and Trojan populations and a 3:2 Kuiper component; no self-gravity or resonance capture.

Pole longitudes and spin phases are illustrative; obliquities and periods use measured values where known. Unknown dwarf-planet tilts are marked. Spheres approximate irregular bodies (Haumea is stretched). Moon counts are the dated NASA March 2025 snapshot, not a live catalog. Temperatures are approximate and refer to different reference levels for terrestrial and giant planets.

Dates use the proleptic Gregorian calendar and Julian day, treating UTC as dynamical time; no leap-second/TDB correction. Fixed elements drift away from real ephemerides over time. Date entry accepts years 0001–9999 for exploration, not precision prediction. Performance depends on viewport, graphics hardware and display refresh rate.
